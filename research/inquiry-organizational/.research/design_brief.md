# Design Brief: Grounding Appreciative Inquiry in Dewey's Logic of Inquiry

> Note on how this brief was produced: the real `research-design-helper`
> skill walks a *human* through 5 Socratic segments and explicitly does
> **not** invent the research question or mechanism. Run solo in this demo,
> segment 1 is pre-filled verbatim from gap G1 (as the skill's contract
> allows when exactly one candidate clears Gates 1-3), but segments 2-4
> are this session's draft placeholders, not a researcher's actual answers
> -- hence `placeholder_segments` in the frontmatter. Do not treat this as
> a finished design brief.

## 1. Research Question (pre-filled from gaps.yml#G1)

Does Appreciative Inquiry's 4-D practice cycle (Discovery, Dream, Design,
Destiny) functionally implement Dewey's pragmatist logic of inquiry
(problematic situation -> observation -> hypothesis formation -> reasoning
-> testing), or does it diverge in ways that explain documented AI
intervention failures outside the healthcare/education contexts where most
AI research to date has concentrated?

## 2. Expected Mechanism *(placeholder -- needs researcher input)*

Draft hypothesis: AI's 4-D cycle front-loads *observation and hypothesis*
(Discovery = observing "what gives life"; Dream = hypothesizing a positive
future) but has no explicit analogue to Dewey's *testing* phase -- Destiny
is commitment/implementation, not falsification. If that asymmetry is real,
it would predict that AI interventions succeed when the "dream" happens to
be right, and have no built-in mechanism to catch it when it isn't -- which
could explain variable outcomes reported outside the well-resourced
healthcare/education settings the 2025 review found AI research
concentrated in.

## 3. Identifiability Check *(placeholder -- needs researcher input)*

Open question the real Socratic dialogue would need to resolve: is "no
testing phase" observable as a *design* property of AI (i.e., visible in
how practitioners run the 4-D cycle), or only inferable post-hoc from
intervention outcomes? If only the latter, the mechanism claim risks being
unfalsifiable without a much larger outcome dataset than a conceptual paper
can gather.

## 4. Validation Plan *(placeholder -- needs researcher input)*

Two options flagged for the researcher to choose between, not decided here:
- **Textual/conceptual validation**: close-read the canonical 4-D
  methodology sources against Dewey's five inquiry phases directly, treat
  the paper's contribution as the mapping + argument.
- **Illustrative case validation**: pair the conceptual mapping with 1-2
  published AI case studies (success and failure) read through the Dewey
  lens, as an existence-proof rather than a generalizable test.
