# Topic Dossier: "inquiry organizational"

This dossier was produced in one session using web search only (no Zotero/

Treat the verdict below as **conditional-go pending a real recall pass** --
see `recall.limitation` in the companion `.gaps.yml`.

## Executive Summary

"Inquiry organizational" resolves to two literatures that sit next to each
other but, in this session's search results, don't cite each other:

1. **Appreciative Inquiry (AI)** as an applied organizational-change
   methodology (strengths-based, 4-D cycle: Discovery, Dream, Design,
   Destiny) -- reviewed at scale in 2024 (Cho & Ardichvili) and 2025
   (Bhatt et al.), both of which flag under-theorized foundations and
   sector/geographic concentration.
2. **Pragmatist "inquiry"** in the Deweyan sense -- a philosophy-of-science
   lens applied to organizational learning and sensemaking (Martela 2015;
   Kelly & Cordeiro 2020; Brandi & Elkjaer 2024; Kuiken 2026).

One candidate gap (G1) sits at their intersection: does AI's 4-D practice
cycle actually implement Dewey's inquiry logic, or is the "inquiry" in
"Appreciative Inquiry" doing rhetorical rather than theoretical work?

**Verdict: conditional-go** (see Gate 3 for the feasible path).

## Gate 1 -- Is the gap still open?

Evidence for open (see `.gaps.yml` G1.evidence_for_open for full citations):
- The 2025 systematic review of 68 Scopus-indexed AI articles names a gap
  in AI's "theoretical anchors" directly.
- The 2024 integrative review across three disciplines (healthcare, higher
  ed, management) synthesizes AI's mechanisms without tracing them to
  Dewey's inquiry stages.
- The pragmatist-inquiry side (Martela; Kelly & Cordeiro; Brandi & Elkjaer)
  builds Deweyan inquiry frameworks for organizational research generally,
  but none of the surfaced abstracts engage AI-as-practice specifically.

**Confidence: low-to-medium.** This rests on 2 search queries plus 4
follow-up author/venue lookups -- not a real recall pass. A paper doing
exactly this cross-walk could exist outside what was indexed by the search
results this session saw (older literature, practitioner journals, or
non-English AI/OD literature weren't checked at all).

## Gate 2 -- Would closing it be a real contribution?

Yes, conditionally: both 2024/2025 reviews already flag "theoretical
anchors" as a named future-research item, which is external validation that
the field itself considers this open and valuable -- not just an artifact
of this session's narrow search. A paper that did the actual stage-by-stage
mapping (Dewey's problematic-situation -> observation -> hypothesis ->
reasoning -> testing against AI's Discovery -> Dream -> Design -> Destiny)
and then showed where they diverge would go beyond restating the reviews'
gap-naming, provided it doesn't stop at the mapping itself and says
something about *why* the divergence matters (e.g., where AI interventions
fail without an explicit falsification/testing step, which Dewey's model
has and AI's 4-D cycle arguably lacks).

## Gate 3 -- Is it feasible?

Two paths, different risk:

- **Conceptual/theoretical paper** (lower risk): synthesize the two
  literatures into an explicit stage-by-stage comparison, argue where they
  align/diverge, propose a revised model. Feasible within a normal
  paper/thesis-chapter timeframe -- no new data collection required.
- **Empirical cross-context study** (higher risk): test whether Dewey-
  inquiry stages predict where practitioner AI interventions succeed or
  fail *outside* the healthcare/education concentration the 2025 review
  flagged. This needs field access to non-education/non-healthcare
  organizations running AI interventions, which is a real recruitment and
  time cost -- PhD-scale, not a single-paper scope.

**Feasibility verdict:** go on the conceptual path; the empirical path is a
separate, larger project that should be scoped on its own after the
conceptual paper exists.
